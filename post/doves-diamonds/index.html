The record moves at 85 BPM in D minor with a regal, mid-tempo sway that never rushes. The production is synth-driven and bass-heavy: atmospheric pads wash over a rolling bassline throughout, crisp hi-hats and hard-hitting drums keep the pulse street-certified, and a piano line layered underneath the chorus gives the track its reflective glow. The mix is polished but keeps the grit intact.

That Boy Hi Hat's flow is measured — half celebration, half memorial — weighing luxury against street narrative, doves against diamonds. His shout-outs to brotherhood land with real weight: tears and scars, all his soldiers, triumph over adversity set against being haunted by the past. The mood is smooth and anthemic, introspective beneath the hype.
