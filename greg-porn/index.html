Biography

Greg Porn is a CWI roster artist in the cyberpunk rap and conscious alt lane, and a co-production artist whose releases are DistroKid-verified under the Cumulative Web Inc copyright (℉ CWI). The cyberpunk rap lane is the sound of the digital underworld: dystopian tension, neon-lit futurism, and an anti-corporate current running underneath the drums. Conscious alt is the lyrical conscience of that sound — introspective, socially aware, and aimed at listeners who want weight with their energy. Greg Porn's catalog sits at the intersection: the aggression and synth-driven atmosphere of cyberpunk rap carrying the reflective, message-first writing of conscious alt. That pairing is what makes the catalog read as both a street-level body of work and a sync-ready asset library.

What defines the Greg Porn catalog is intent. "Pay Yourself" was conceived as a mental-health-awareness anthem — a track whose theme is self-investment and personal wellbeing. Standing law across the CWI catalog: every track is co-produced by Black Lansky. "Pay Yourself" adds producer Rick Friedrich to that equation — produced by Rick Friedrich, co-produced by Black Lansky — while "On Edge" and "Unholy Hunter" carry Black Lansky's co-production as the connective thread across all three songs.

The Music

The Greg Porn sound is cyberpunk rap with a conscious alt conscience. The cyberpunk rap half supplies the atmosphere: dystopian tension, digital grit, and the aggressive forward motion that makes the music trailer-ready. The conscious alt half supplies the message: introspection, social awareness, and themes — like mental health — that give the energy something to mean. Neither half works without the other in this catalog; the atmosphere without the message is noise, and the message without the atmosphere doesn't move.

"Pay Yourself" is the clearest statement of that balance. As a mental-health-awareness anthem, it takes one of the heaviest subjects in modern life — the pressure to keep producing while falling apart — and turns it into a directive: invest in yourself first. The theme works because it is not abstract advice; it is framed as the song's moral core, the anti-corporate insistence that a person's wellbeing outranks the machine demanding their output. That is what makes it an anthem rather than a song: it gives language to a struggle a whole audience shares, and CWI's published material treats it as the catalog's broadest-resonance asset for exactly that reason — documentary, cinematic trailer, or commercial placements built around personal journey, mental health, or overcoming struggle.

"On Edge" and "Unholy Hunter" carry the same DNA into darker rooms. "On Edge" channels the conscious-alt introspection inward until it becomes psychological tension — the sound of a mind under pressure, which is why it reads as a thriller asset. "Unholy Hunter" channels the cyberpunk rap aggression outward into pure spectacle — the sound of a world under pressure, which is why it reads as a gaming trailer hook. Three tracks, three pressure points: the self, the psyche, the world.

Sync & Licensing

Two of the three Greg Porn tracks were conceived as sync assets, and CWI's published material maps their targets explicitly. "On Edge" is positioned as a psychological-thriller sync asset: lyrical density, sustained tension, and a mood built for suspense scenes, deep character exposition, and sophisticated television — the neo-noir and psychological-drama lane where music has to function as part of the narrative, not background to it.

"Unholy Hunter" is positioned as a dystopian gaming trailer hook: aggressive, synth-driven, cinematic — a high-energy cyberpunk anthem aimed at game audio directors and trailer editors. CWI's published sync guide names its targets as fast-paced trailers, level introductions, and high-stakes combat sequences, and frames the broader catalog as answering the demand for atmospheric, urban-futuristic sound in gaming — including the kind of dystopian tension and digital grit that titles like Cyberpunk 2077 trade in.

"Pay Yourself" completes the sync picture as the emotional core: a mid-tempo, introspective track with driving drums and a heavy, reflective atmosphere, positioned for moments that require emotional weight — documentaries, cinematic trailers, and commercials focused on personal journey and mental health. Together the three tracks give a supervisor three distinct, fully cleared CWI-controlled assets: the anthem, the thriller, and the trailer hook.
