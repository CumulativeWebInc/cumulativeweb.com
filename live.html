The stream is the front row

Cumulative Web Inc programs live video as a standing channel: shows, studio behind-the-scenes, and conversations with the scene. The philosophy is the same one behind Signal Sessions — the room is the studio, and the stream is the front row. If you can’t be in the DMV, you don’t miss the moment.

Live programming runs in seasons around the release calendar. The current season is built around Cyberpunk 2027 — That Boy Hi Hat’s October 20, 2026 single: studio sessions, the video production diary, and release-week programming. Between seasons, the channel carries catalog listening sessions, producer breakdowns, and Q&As with the roster.

What streams

Shows and sessions. Live performances and Signal Sessions recordings, multicam where the venue allows. The pilot — a Rock-Rap Night built around 183 Wildboi — set the template: a real show, captured properly, streamed live.

Studio behind-the-scenes. Tracking, mixing, and mastering at Cue Recording Studios in Arlington, Virginia — the room behind That Boy Hi Hat’s flagship records. Watch records get built, not just released.

Conversations. The label’s extended circle — producers (Jeck Da General, Hybrid, Kokurcho), engineers (Blaine Misner), curators, and visual artists — talking process, not promo.

Radio 365 never sleeps

When nothing is streaming live, Radio 365 carries the 24/7 audio: the full CWI catalog, alternative rap and post-trap futurism around the clock. Live video and Radio 365 are the two halves of the broadcast operation — watch the moment, or listen to the catalog.

Frequently asked

Where do I watch?

Streams are announced on this page and the label’s social channels (YouTube @cumulativeweb, Instagram @cumulativeweb, X @cumulativeweb) with the watch link. Major streams are also archived for replay.

Can I perform or appear on a stream?

Signal Sessions slots and guest conversations are booked through the contact page (event-booking path). Bring a live history and a reason the audience should care.

Do streams cost anything?

No — live programming is free to watch. It’s a discovery channel: the label would rather have the audience than the ticket revenue.
