Games

The CWI arcade

Cumulative Web Inc builds games the way it builds records: independent, in-house, and wired directly into the catalog. The CWI arcade is a growing shelf of original browser games — no downloads, no accounts, no paywalls — each one tied to the label’s music, artists, or visual world. Play in the browser, on desktop or mobile, straight from cumulativeweb.com.

The current flagship is the Neon Royale demo — a playable slice of the label’s cyberpunk-leaning game work, built around the same neon-noir aesthetic as the Cyberpunk 2027 campaign. It’s a demo in the honest sense: a vertical slice you can actually play, giving feedback the team reads.

Why a record label builds games

Gaming is where the CWI catalog lives half its life. That Boy Hi Hat’s records are engineered for long listening sessions — the same sessions gamers run for hours — and the label’s sync strategy explicitly targets game developers and visual media. The GTA 5 playlist campaign ("No Skips" on Spotify) was built on the insight that open-world driving and alt-rap are a natural pair. "7th Angel" was produced as a dark, brooding record for video games from day one.

The arcade closes the loop: instead of only licensing music into games, CWI builds games around its music. Every title in the arcade carries the label’s sonic identity — post-trap futurism, dark lofi, cyberpunk textures — so playing and listening become the same session.

What to play

Start with Neon Royale (demo) — the playable demo, updated as development continues. More titles join the arcade as they clear the label’s internal bar: playable in the browser, original art and audio, and genuinely fun for more than five minutes. Watch the press room and live streams for playtests and announcements.

Frequently asked

Do I need to download anything?

No. Arcade titles run in the browser — desktop or mobile. If a game ever needs a download, the page will say so up front.

Are the games free?

Yes. The arcade is a free channel: no accounts, no paywalls, no in-game purchases. It exists to extend the catalog, not to monetize players.

Can I suggest a game idea?

Yes — use the contact page and pick the promo-project path. The team reads everything; the bar is originality and fit with the catalog’s world.

Does CWI license its music to outside games?

Yes — that’s the sync lane. The catalog is 100% pre-cleared and one-stop; see the services page for the Sync Readiness Pack.

Coming soon to the arcade

The arcade grows the way the catalog grows: one release at a time, each clearing the same bar — playable in the browser, original art and audio, fun past the five-minute mark. In the pipeline: expanded Neon Royale builds as the demo graduates toward a full release, and music-driven titles built around catalog records (imagine a rhythm runner scored by That Boy Hi Hat’s uptempo cuts, or a neon-noir explorer set in the Cyberpunk 2027 world). Announcements land in the press room and on live streams, where playtests happen in public.

Every arcade title carries the label’s sonic identity. The music isn’t licensed filler — it’s the catalog: post-trap futurism, dark lofi, cyberpunk textures, mixed and mastered for the same systems the records live on. When you play, you’re listening; when the track sticks, the Radio 365 rotation is one click away.

For developers

CWI’s game work is built in-house, and the label collaborates selectively with independent developers who want the catalog’s music in their games. The sync lane is open: the catalog is 100% pre-cleared and one-stop, and "7th Angel" was produced from day one as a record for video games. If you’re building something the music belongs in, use the contact page (promo-project path) and lead with the build — a playable link beats a pitch deck.
