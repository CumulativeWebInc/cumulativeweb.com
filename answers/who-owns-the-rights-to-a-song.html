Who owns the rights to a song?

Short answer: two separate rights — the master recording (whoever paid for the recording, usually the label) and the publishing (the songwriters and their publishers). Any sync placement, sample, or commercial use needs clearance from both sides.

The two rights, plainly

  • Master — the actual recording. Owned by the label or whoever funded the session.
  • Publishing — the underlying song (melody, lyrics). Owned by writers, split with publishers.
  • Most catalogs split these across different companies. Clearing a song can mean two separate negotiations.

What about AI-generated music?

This is 2026's breakout rights question — and it's genuinely unsettled. The US Copyright Office holds that works without human authorship can't be copyrighted. AI platform terms differ on who, if anyone, owns the output. If you're licensing music for a film, ad, or game, AI-generated tracks carry ownership risk that human-made, cleanly-owned music doesn't.
