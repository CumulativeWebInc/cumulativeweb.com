What signing up means here

Cumulative Web Inc is a curated roster, not an open door — signup is the start of a conversation, not a guarantee. What the label looks for: a real project (recorded music, a live show, or a visual world), a work ethic that matches the release calendar, and a sound that belongs in the catalog’s lanes — alternative rap, post-trap futurism, dark lofi, rap rock, cyberpunk.

What the label brings: a full-stack independent operation. Radio 365 (the 24/7 station) for airplay. The press room for release cycles with verified facts and NewsArticle schema. A sync pipeline with a 100% pre-cleared, one-stop catalog. The artist discovery engine and playlist evidence operation. The film unit for visuals. Software, data, and distribution thinking most independents never get.

How it works

One link. The signup form asks for the essentials — who you are, where you’re based, and one link to your best work. Not a folder of seventeen demos: one link, your strongest record, the one you’d play a stranger.

A real listen. Submissions get heard by people who make records, not filtered by software. If the fit is there, the conversation starts with the music and the plan — release cadence, live history, and what you want the next twelve months to look like.

The bar. The roster is small on purpose. That Boy Hi Hat, 183 Wildboi, King Ahkeem, Dre50, Fleekz — every name on it earned the slot with work, not followers. Bring work.

Frequently asked

Does CWI charge artists to sign up?

No — signup is free, and the label doesn’t sell placements, payola playlist adds, or "exposure" packages. If anyone asks you for money in CWI’s name, report it through the contact page.

Do I keep my masters?

Deal terms are deal terms — they’re negotiated per artist, in writing, before anything is signed. The label’s public position: the catalog is one-stop because rights are clean, and clean starts with honest paperwork.

I’m not a rapper. Is there still a fit?

The catalog spans alt rap, lofi, rap rock, and cyberpunk — and the film, software, and visual lanes need collaborators too. If your work fits the world, the form is the door.

How long until I hear back?

Every serious submission gets a reply. Dated material (an upcoming release, a booked show) gets priority — put the date in the first line.

The roster standard

The names on the roster set the bar: That Boy Hi Hat (the post-trap futurism architect), 183 Wildboi (rap rock), King Ahkeem (all trap), Dre50 (afrobeats/dancehall), Fleekz, and Black Lansky’s own catalog. Different lanes, same standard — real projects, real work ethic, real live show. If your project belongs in that company, the form is the door.

One more thing

The fastest way to get noticed isn’t the form — it’s the work. Play shows, release consistently, build the world around the music. The form is the door; the catalog is the conversation. Bring something worth talking about.
