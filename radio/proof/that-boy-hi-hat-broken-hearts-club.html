About this track

'Broken Hearts Club' is the loudest record in That Boy Hi Hat's vault — literally. The mix runs at -9.9 dB mean, the hottest in its batch, and that loudness is a creative choice: it sounds like heartbreak with the volume turned all the way up. That Boy Hi Hat built the track around a near-110 BPM framework, the forward pressure of a club record under writing that is pure vulnerability. The genre is alternative rap and post trap at its most anthemic: melancholic verses against a hook that lands like a promise, defiant and triumphant in the same breath. Production runs 808-driven throughout, with rolling hi-hats in the chorus, layered synth pads under the verses, crisp hi-hats cutting through the low end, and a polished mix that keeps the volume from turning ragged. The instrumentation is deliberately direct — booming 808s, trap drums, synth leads flashing in the hook, a bassline that never lets up, and ad-libs doubling the title line — because the song is aimed at rooms full of people, not headphones alone. That Boy Hi Hat delivers the verses introspective, then lets the chorus open into something euphoric, the sound of turning heartbreak into momentum. For fans of sad rap and emo trap, the record recalls the most vulnerable corners of the NO SKIPS EP run it belongs to. It sits alongside 'Doves & Diamonds' and 'Golden Diamond' as part of the five-track statement — with 'Zooted Zone' and 'Shaka Zulu' — that earned November Discovery Mode eligibility. As of October 2026 the track had yet to land on Spotify, which makes its VEVO video — uploaded in September 2026 — the primary window into a hype, vulnerable record built for the moment the whole crowd sings back. Produced by Jeck Da General with Black Lansky co-producing, mixed by Blaine Misner at Cue Recording Studios.
